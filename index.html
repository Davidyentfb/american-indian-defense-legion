Defensive knowledge.

    
Concept-level education — the Legion describes defenses, never attacks. Nothing here instructs wrongdoing.

    

      What ethical hacking is
      

        
The authorized testing of systems, networks, and applications to find vulnerabilities before malicious actors do. The entire discipline rests on one pillar: explicit, documented authorization. Without it, the same act is a crime.

        
    
          
  • White hat — works with authorization: penetration testers, security researchers, red teams. The Legion’s posture: white hat only.
    • 
          
  • Penetration testing — scoped, time-boxed simulated attacks against agreed targets.
    • 
          
  • Vulnerability assessment — systematic discovery and ranking of weaknesses.
    • 
          
  • Red teaming — full-scope adversary simulation, including consented social engineering.
    • 
        

      

    

    

      Defensive fundamentals for small organizations
      

        
    
          
  • OPSEC — control what information leaves the organization and how.
    • 
          
  • Least privilege — most data theft involves someone with legitimate access; limit access to what each role needs.
    • 
          
  • Supply-chain caution — vendors and software providers are attack paths; vet them.
    • 
          
  • Incident-response readiness — know who to call and what to preserve before an incident, so reporting is fast and clean.
    • 
        

      

    

    

      Counter-espionage awareness (defensive perspective)
      

        
Understanding how espionage works so you can defend against it — not participate in it. Relevant forms include quiet cyber intrusion aimed at stealing data, theft of trade secrets through insiders or compromised vendors, and misappropriation of traditional knowledge and cultural designs. Defense is policy, access control, and vigilance — never retaliation.

      

    

  







  

    
About the Legion

    
Why the Legion exists.

    
Crimes against American Indian communities — on the land, on the ledger, and in the feed — are under-documented and under-defended. Scams target Native communities. “Indian-made” fraud undercuts Native artists. Cyber harassment and exploitation cross into Indian Country with too little recourse. Tribal organizations face the same cyber threats as any enterprise, usually without a security team. The American Indian Defense Legion was founded to close that gap: a cybersecurity company that receives the reports, documents the patterns, defends the organizations, and protects the assets — from land to art to digital holdings.

    

      
Defense onlyWe find weaknesses and document harm. We do not attack, retaliate, or operate outside authorization.

      
Authorization firstEvery assessment is scoped in writing before it begins. No engagement, no testing.

      
Consent-driven reportingReports are referred to authorities only with the reporter’s explicit consent.

      
Data sovereignty respectedTribal control of tribal data — privacy, anonymity options, and clear retention practices.
